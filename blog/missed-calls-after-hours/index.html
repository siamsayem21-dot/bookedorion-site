What really happens when your HVAC shop misses a call after hours

A homeowner with a dead AC rarely leaves a voicemail. Here is what happens to that call, what it costs, and four ways to stop losing it.

Siam AhmedFounder, BookedOrion

It’s 9:47 PM in July. A homeowner in Plano walks into a hot house, finds the AC blowing warm air and calls the first HVAC shop on Google. Your phone rings. Nobody answers. What happens next decides whether you get that job.

Most people don’t leave a voicemail

When a call goes to voicemail, most callers hang up. One call-handling company looked at call data from 10,000 businesses and found that 69% of callers who reach voicemail don’t leave a message (Moneypenny, 2016). The same report found that a third of small businesses surveyed didn’t answer incoming calls at all.

That data is a few years old and isn’t HVAC-specific. But ask any homeowner with no cooling what they do when a shop doesn’t pick up. They don’t wait. They call the next number.

The after-hours call is usually a real job

Nobody calls an HVAC company at 9 PM to chat. After-hours calls tend to be the urgent ones: no cooling, no heat, a strange noise, water under the air handler. These callers are ready to book. That’s exactly why losing them hurts.

Four ways to stop losing these calls

1. Forward to your own cell

Free, and it works until you’re on a roof, under a house or asleep. It also means you never really clock out.

2. A live answering service

A person answers, which callers like. Many services take a message instead of booking the job, and per-minute pricing climbs fast in summer. We compare costs in this guide.

3. A night-shift CSR

The best experience, and the most expensive. Most small shops can’t justify a full-time person for nights and weekends.

4. An AI receptionist

Answers every call in seconds, any hour, and can book straight onto your calendar. It should say it’s an AI, follow your rules for emergencies and hand tricky callers to a human. That’s how Molly works.

A simple fix you can do tonight

  • Change your voicemail greeting to name your company and say when you’ll call back.
  • Write down which calls count as emergencies (start with our triage list).
  • Count how many calls you miss this week. Your phone’s call log has the answer.
