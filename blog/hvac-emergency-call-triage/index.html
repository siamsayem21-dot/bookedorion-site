Emergency or not? A simple call triage list for HVAC shops

SABy Siam Ahmed, Founder of BookedOrion

Not every after-hours call is an emergency, and treating them all the same either burns out your techs or leaves a family in danger. Here’s a simple list you can give your office, your answering service or your AI receptionist.

Safety first. For a gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm, the caller’s first step is never “wait for a tech.” Tell them to get out of the house and call 911 from outside.

Level 1: Life safety. Act now.

Gas smell

Tell the caller to leave the area right away, then call 911 and their gas company from a safe distance. In most of North Texas that’s Atmos Energy, whose 24-hour emergency line is 866-322-8667. Then alert the owner or on-call tech.

Carbon monoxide alarm sounding

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission says never ignore a CO alarm: get outside immediately and call 911 from fresh air. Don’t go back in until responders say it’s safe.

Burning smell, smoke or sparks from equipment

Tell the caller to turn the system off at the thermostat and call 911 if there is smoke or fire. Alert the on-call tech.

Level 2: Urgent. Same night or first thing.

  • No heat in freezing weather, especially with elderly people, babies or someone who is ill in the home.
  • No cooling during extreme heat with vulnerable people inside. Texas health officials list older adults, young children and people with chronic illness as most at risk from heat, and say air conditioning is the best protection.
  • Water leaking from the unit onto floors or ceilings.

Your rule here is a business choice: ring the on-call tech now, or book the first slot in the morning. Write it down so everyone handles it the same way.

Level 3: Book it. Next available slot.

  • AC running but not cooling well, in normal weather
  • Strange noises with the system still working
  • Thermostat problems
  • Tune-ups and maintenance visits
  • New system quotes
  • Price questions

Turn this into a one-page rule sheet

  1. List your Level 1 and Level 2 words: “gas,” “smell,” “alarm,” “smoke,” “no heat,” “leak.”
  2. Decide who gets the call for each level, and their number.
  3. Write the exact words to tell the caller for gas and CO.
  4. Share it with everyone who answers your phone.

This is the same list we set up with every BookedOrion shop. You choose the emergency words, and Molly rings you first when she hears one.

This article is general information, not professional safety advice. Follow your local gas utility, fire department and code requirements.
