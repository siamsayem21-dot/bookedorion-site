Summer rush phone checklist for HVAC owners

The first heat wave brings more calls than your office can handle. Seven things to set up before it hits, so no booking slips through.

Siam AhmedFounder, BookedOrion

The first real heat wave in Dallas–Fort Worth changes your phone overnight. Calls double, techs are booked out and the office can’t keep up. A little setup in spring keeps those calls from turning into lost jobs.

1. Fix your voicemail greeting

Say your company name, that you got their call, and when you’ll call back. “You’ve reached the voicemail of…” sounds like nobody works there.

2. Decide what happens after hours

Forward to an on-call phone, an answering service or an AI receptionist. Pick one and write it down. “We’ll figure it out” means voicemail.

3. Write your emergency list

Which calls get someone out tonight? No cooling with an elderly person at home, water leaking through a ceiling, a burning smell. Our triage guide has a starting list.

4. Set a callback promise, and keep it

“We call back within one hour during business hours” is a promise customers remember, especially if you keep it.

5. Agree on what to say about price

Everyone answering your phone should give the same diagnostic or trip fee, and nobody should guess a repair price over the phone.

6. Count your missed calls once a week

Your phone’s call log shows them. If the number climbs in June, it’s time to add coverage before July.

7. Make booking easy

Keep open slots visible to whoever answers, so they can book on the call instead of promising a callback.
