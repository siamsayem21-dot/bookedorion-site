How much does an AI receptionist cost for an HVAC shop?

The four ways AI receptionists are priced, the costs hiding in the fine print, and how to work out what you would really pay per booked job.

Siam AhmedFounder, BookedOrion

Most owners ask about price first, and that’s fair. But the monthly number on a pricing page rarely tells you what you’ll pay in July, when the phone doesn’t stop. Here’s how AI receptionist pricing works, what to watch for, and how to work out your real cost.

The short answer

An AI receptionist usually costs a monthly fee plus something tied to use: minutes, calls or booked jobs. BookedOrion, for example, is $79 a month plus $29 per booked job, never more than $449 in a month, or $249 a month flat with unlimited bookings. Whatever you compare, the number that matters most is your cost per booked job.

Four ways AI receptionists are priced

Pricing modelHow it worksWatch out for
Flat monthlyOne price with a set number of minutesWhat happens when a busy month goes over
Per minuteYou pay for talk timeLong calls and summer spikes push the bill up
Per callYou pay for each answered callWhether spam, hang-ups and wrong numbers count
Base plus per booked jobA small base, then a charge when a job lands on your calendarHow “booked” is defined, and what happens when a customer cancels

No model is best for everyone. It depends on how many calls you get and how much that changes from spring to July. A per-minute plan can look cheap in April and expensive in a heat wave. A flat plan is easy to budget but you pay the same in a slow month.

Costs that hide in the fine print

  • Setup fees. Some charge to build your script and connect your calendar.
  • Contracts. An annual term or a long cancellation notice.
  • Overage rates. What each extra minute or call costs once your allowance runs out.
  • Spam. Whether robocalls and wrong numbers eat your minutes or count as calls.
  • Add-ons. Calendar booking, text alerts, recordings or a second number sold separately.
  • Your time. Self-serve software you have to set up, test and tune yourself.

Ask for each of these in writing before you start a trial.

Work out your cost per booked job

Take what you’d pay in a month and divide it by the jobs it books for you. That one number lets you compare any two options, and compare them with what a job is worth to you.

How that compares to hiring someone

The median pay for receptionists in the US was $18.27 an hour, or $38,010 a year, in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. That’s before payroll taxes and benefits, and it covers one shift. Answering nights, weekends and holidays would take more than one person.

A good person brings things software can’t: judgment, warmth and knowing your regular customers. That’s why many shops do both. Someone answers by day, and an AI receptionist takes the calls they miss and everything after hours.

Five questions to ask before you sign up

  • What would a month twice as busy cost me?
  • Do spam and wrong-number calls count toward my bill?
  • What counts as a booked job, and what if the customer cancels?
  • Is there a setup fee, a contract or a cancellation notice?
  • Can I listen to the recording of every call?
